Example 1  ·  of 4

Three quotes, one table

One of you described this in the form: collect quotations from different emails and files, then merge them into Excel by hand. Two to three hours.

Why this one first. You are not bad at Excel. The slow part is that the numbers arrive in different shapes — one in the email body, one in a PDF, one in a chat message. Copying them into the same shape is the work. AI is good at exactly that.
You do not need to tidy anything first. Messy is fine. Paste it as it came.

Example 2  ·  of 4

Ask the table a question

Now the table exists. The real question was never "what are the prices". It was "which one should we take".

Why this matters. In Excel this is where you start building formulas and get it wrong at 6pm. Here you just ask, in your own words, in the same conversation.
Stay in the same chat. Do not start a new one. Type this.
What you type
We need 300,000 pieces delivered to Manila.

1. Which suppliers can actually take this order? Say why for each.
2. For the ones that can, show the landed cost per piece including freight if freight is stated.
3. Show your calculation for each one, line by line.
4. If a supplier cannot be compared fairly because something is missing, say so instead of estimating.
✓ This is what success looks like
It tells you Greenfield is out at 300,000 pieces because its MOQ is 500,000. It shows the arithmetic for the other two instead of only giving you a winner. Where freight is not stated, it says so rather than inventing a figure.
Why we ask for the calculation. Fifteen of you wrote in the form that you worry the answer may be wrong and you will not know. Making it show the working is how you check it in ten seconds instead of trusting it blindly.
Now with your own Ask it the question you actually have to answer for your boss this week. Use your own words. You do not need special wording.
Example 3  ·  of 4

Find what is missing

The expensive mistakes are not wrong numbers. They are the gaps nobody noticed until the PO was already out.

Why this one. One of you reads every section of a bidding document and takes notes, one to two hours. Another compares the inventory sheet against ERP line by line. Both are the same job: looking for the thing that does not match.
Same chat again. Type this.
What you type
Before we send a PO, check the three quotes again and list:

1. Anything a supplier did not state that we would need before ordering.
2. Anything that is inconsistent between the three, for example different Incoterms that make the prices not comparable.
3. Any term that puts risk on us.

Give it as a short list. One line each. Do not repeat the table.
✓ This is what success looks like
A short list, not another table. It points out that the three prices are FOB, EXW and CIF and therefore not directly comparable; that Sunrise wants an LC; and that only Greenfield stated freight.
The one-line version to remember. "What is missing, what does not match, what puts risk on us." That works on quotes, on bidding documents, on inventory reports, on contracts.
Now with your own Take a document you are checking this week. Paste it. Ask the same three questions. See what it catches that you had not written down yet.
Example 4  ·  of 4

Get it back into Excel

The table is on screen. Your report lives in Excel. This is the step people usually lose twenty minutes on.

Why it goes wrong. If you copy a table straight off the screen, Excel drops everything into column A and you start splitting cells by hand. Asking for tab-separated text avoids that completely.
Type this, then copy the block it gives you and paste into a blank Excel sheet.
What you type
Give me the comparison table again as tab-separated text, with a header row, nothing else.
No bullet points, no explanation, no markdown. Just the rows.
✓ This is what success looks like
You paste into cell A1 and the data lands in separate columns on its own. Supplier in A, unit price in B, and so on. No Text-to-Columns, no manual splitting.
If it still lands in one column. Your Excel may be set to expect commas. Go back and ask for it as CSV instead, then use Data → Text to Columns once. After that it will behave.
Now with your own Do the whole thing end to end with your own emails: paste the replies, get the table, ask your question, pull it into Excel. That round trip is the whole skill.

You can take information that arrived in four different shapes and have one clean table in about two minutes. One of you wrote that this takes two to three hours today.
Example 1  ·  of 4

Reply from bullet points

One of you wrote in the form: "Every time I need to email, I first have to recheck the grammar, then again and again, and it takes about 1 hour."

Why this is slow. You are doing two jobs at once. You are deciding what to say and worrying about how it sounds in the same pass. That is why you rewrite the same sentence four times. Separate them: you decide the facts, it does the wording.
Write your bullets in whatever English you would use with a colleague. It does not need to be correct.
Copy the instruction, replace the bullets with your own facts, send it.
What you type
Write a reply email to a supplier. Here are the facts:

- we received their quotation, thank them
- price is higher than last order, we need them to explain the increase
- we need delivery before 20 October, ask if that is possible
- we need the freight cost separately, it was not in their quote
- we will confirm the PO once these are clear

Keep it short and businesslike. No flowery language.
Do not add anything I did not give you.
✓ This is what success looks like
A short email comes back with all five points in it and nothing extra. No invented delivery date, no made-up order number. If you count five points in the draft, it worked.
The line that keeps it honest. "Do not add anything I did not give you." Without it, drafts quietly grow a sentence you never approved.
Now with your own Open the email you have been putting off since yesterday. Write five bullets. Paste them in. Compare the time.
Example 2  ·  of 4

Change the tone, not the content

The draft is right but it reads too soft. Or too hard. Normally you rewrite the whole thing.

Why you rewrite it now. Tone lives in the verbs and the hedges, not in the facts. Changing it by hand means touching every sentence. Asking for it takes one line and the facts stay locked.
Stay in the same chat, right after the draft. Try all three and read them side by side.
What you type
Give me three versions of that same email. Same facts, no additions.

1. Neutral and factual.
2. Firmer, we have said this before and we need a date this time.
3. Warmer, this is a supplier we want to keep.

Keep all three under 120 words.
✓ This is what success looks like
Three versions appear. Version 2 says something close to "please confirm the date by Friday". Version 3 still asks for the same date but opens differently. The five facts are in all three.
Read them out loud in your head before picking. The one you would be comfortable receiving is usually the right one.
Now with your own Take a reply you already sent last week that got no answer. Ask for the firmer version. That is often the reason it got no answer.
Example 3  ·  of 4

Did I answer everything they asked

One of you wrote: "need to check if every detail is included and correct". This is that check, done in ten seconds.

Why this one earns its place. The email that costs you a second round trip is not the one with bad grammar. It is the one that answered three of their four questions.
Paste the email you received, then your draft reply, then ask.
What you type
Here is the email I received:
[paste their email]

Here is my draft reply:
[paste your draft]

1. List every question or request in their email.
2. For each one, say whether my draft answers it. Yes or no.
3. List anything I answered that they did not actually ask.

Do not rewrite my draft. Just the check.
✓ This is what success looks like
A numbered list of their questions with Yes or No beside each. If there is a No, you found the second round trip before it happened.
Works on more than email. Same three questions work on a bidding requirement list, an audit request, or a customer complaint.
Now with your own Use the longest email in your inbox right now. The one with four questions buried in one paragraph.
Example 4  ·  of 4

Stop re-explaining yourself

By now you have typed "keep it short and businesslike" four times. There is a place to say it once.

Why it matters more than it looks. Most people give up on AI in week two, not week one. They give up because every conversation starts from zero and re-typing the context is more work than doing the task. This is the fix, and it takes two minutes.
In ChatGPT, open the menu at your name, bottom left. Choose Customise ChatGPT. Paste the block below into the second box and save.
Paste into "How would you like ChatGPT to respond?"
I work in a medical supplies manufacturing company in the Philippines.
Most of my writing is business email to suppliers and customers.

When you draft anything for me:
- Keep it short and businesslike. No flowery language.
- Never add facts, dates, quantities or names I did not give you.
  If something is missing, ask me instead of filling it in.
- Use plain English. Short sentences.
- When I give you numbers, keep them exactly as written.

When I ask you to compare or calculate, show the working line by line.
✓ This is what success looks like
Save it, open a brand new chat, and type just "draft a reply asking the supplier for the freight cost". The reply comes back short and businesslike without you saying so. That is the setting working.
Edit the first two lines. Change "medical supplies manufacturing" and the description of your writing to match your own department. That is the whole customisation.

Example 1  ·  of 3

Write your card

One of you asked for this in the form: a personalised setup that fits your own work tasks. This is how you get it without paying.

What a card is. One block of text that describes your job, your data, and how you want the answer. You keep it in a note or a Word file. You paste it at the top of a new chat and everything after it behaves. It is the difference between a tool you use twice and a tool you use daily.
Fill in the four blanks. Use your own task from the pre-session form.
Your card — fill in the four blanks
MY TASK CARD

Who I am:
I work in [your department] at Medtecs Philippines.

What I will give you:
[what the raw material is, for example: supplier quotations pasted
from different emails / inventory lines exported from ERP /
a weekly update from three team members]

What I want back:
[the finished thing, for example: one comparison table /
a short summary of what changed / a draft email]

Rules:
- Keep every number exactly as given. Never convert or round.
- If something is missing, write "not stated". Never guess.
- When you compare or calculate, show the working.
- Short sentences. No flowery language.
✓ This is what success looks like
You can read your own card out loud and it describes what you actually do on a Tuesday. If the middle two blanks are vague, go back to your pre-session form and copy the sentence you wrote there.
Where to keep it. A note on your phone, a Word file on your desktop, or the bottom of your own draft email to yourself. Anywhere you can copy from in five seconds.
Now with your own Write a second card for your other regular task. Most people end up with two or three, not one.
